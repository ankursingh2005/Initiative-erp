Your Incentive Guide

Initiative ERP / IDSPL | A simple guide to every calculation sheet

This guide explains how your monthly sales become an incentive amount, how the IDS Fund is built, and how that fund is shared with support teams.

Start with three questions

How much did the outlet sell? The uploaded report provides the sales amount.

How much sales incentive does that produce? The system estimates profit, calculates a base incentive, then applies the outlet's payout share.

How much goes into the IDS Fund? A separate set of category rates builds the fund used for non-sales staff allocations.

A useful distinction
Base incentive, exact sales payout and IDS Fund are three different amounts. Give each amount its own name when discussing a report, so everyone knows which calculation you mean.

The seven sheets you will see

Sheet nameWhat it helps you understand
Outlet SummaryThe basic calculation for each outlet
Category IncentiveThe basic incentive for each main category
Grouped IncentiveSales and incentive for each category/group in the upload
Sales IncentiveThe sales payout after applying the outlet/group share

How to use this guide

Read the basic example first, then follow the sheet that you need to check. All amounts in the examples are in rupees and use made-up sales figures. They are not actual employee payments.

Get the Sales File Ready

01 / A good upload makes the calculation easier to trust

Before you upload

  1. Choose the month or reporting period you want to calculate.
  2. Prepare an Excel .xlsx / .xlsm file, or a PDF with readable text.
  3. Check the outlet, category and sales amount in each row.
  4. Make sure the same sales are not repeated on several sheets.
  5. After uploading, compare the system's sales totals with your original report.

Names the system recognizes

Outlet nameShort name in the report
AlambaghALM
AshiyanaASH
HazratganjHZT
GomtinagarGNG
Vikas NagarVKN

The incentive calculation uses these five outlets. Rows for other outlets are excluded from the calculation.

Category codes are HA, HE, MOB, COM, DC, ACC and ACC COM. The system recognizes ACC COM separately from COM and ACC.

Small details that affect totals

One period per file is easiest to check. The system can read a month field, but its calculation combines all uploaded months. It does not produce a separate incentive total for each month.

Repeated rows need attention. Identical parsed rows can be removed within a single sheet. Repeated sales on different sheets can still be added together.

Excel formulas need saved results. The importer reads the last saved formula values. Open and save the file in Excel first if those values are missing or outdated.

Check PDFs carefully. This importer reads text, not scanned pictures. On a matching line, it takes the last number as the sales amount. A line with several amounts may need checking.

The parser prefers recognized detail rows over total rows when details exist. Uploaded applied percentages or payout amounts do not replace the fixed payout rules used by the sales calculation.

Numbers in brackets are treated as negative. Currency symbols and commas are removed when reading amounts. Rate parsing treats values from 0 to 1 as fractions, so numeric 1 becomes 100%.

From Sales to Base Incentive

02 / Outlet Summary, Category Incentive and Grouped Incentive

Imagine your outlet sold Rs. 100,000 during the month. Here is how the basic calculation works at the default rates.

Step 1: estimate the average profit

Rs. 100,000 × 7% = Rs. 7,000

The 7% is an editable estimate used for incentives. It is not a calculation from the actual cost of each product.

Step 2: calculate the base incentive

Rs. 7,000 × 2.5% = Rs. 175

The 2.5% rate is also editable. The system rounds profit to two decimal places first, then calculates and rounds the base incentive.

Where the same calculation appears

SheetWhat sales are used?
Outlet SummaryAll accepted sales for one outlet
Category IncentiveSales for one outlet and one of the six main categories
Grouped IncentiveSales for one outlet/category group in the upload

Why category totals can differ

The Category Incentive sheet lists HA, HE, MOB, COM, DC and ACC. It does not list ACC COM. Outlet Summary and Grouped Incentive can include ACC COM.

For example, Rs. 100,000 in each of all seven categories gives outlet sales of Rs. 700,000 and a base incentive of Rs. 1,225. The six-category calculation uses Rs. 600,000 and produces Rs. 1,050. The Rs. 175 difference belongs to ACC COM.

When you edit a downloaded sheet
These three sheets contain calculated amounts, rather than formulas that update every value. Changing a sales cell does not automatically update its profit or incentive. Re-upload corrected data to obtain a fresh calculation.

Grand totals are calculated from combined sales, rather than by adding all rounded row incentives. A small rounding difference can therefore be expected. The exported category sheet lists category rows, without the API's category-total row.

How the IDS Fund Is Built

04 / A separate category-based calculation

The IDS Fund uses fixed category rates. The editable 7% profit and 2.5% base incentive rates do not drive this calculation.

Two steps for each category

1. Category sales × Category rate = Category incentive
2. Category incentive × Fund share = IDS Fund contribution
CategoryALM / ASH / GNG / VKN rateHZT rateFund share
MOB0.125%0.125%30%
COM0.25%0.25%20%
DC0.25%0.25%25%
HA0.25%0.25%20%

A mobile-sales example

If mobile sales are Rs. 100,000, the category incentive is Rs. 100,000 × 0.125% = Rs. 125. The fund receives 30% of Rs. 125, which is Rs. 37.50.

Why HZT's accessories contribution is different

For Rs. 100,000 in ACC sales, ALM contributes Rs. 1,000 × 25% = Rs. 250. HZT contributes Rs. 500 × 25% = Rs. 125 because its ACC rate is 0.5%.

Ware House uses the combined fund

Rs. 3,500 × 16% = Rs. 560.00 for Ware House

The warehouse amount appears on a separate MWH row. It is calculated once from the combined fund. Outlet allocations are rounded to whole rupees; Ware House is rounded to two decimals.

These example department amounts total Rs. 3,128. The system does not automatically assign the remaining fund balance to a department.

If one outlet is missing
The backend leaves that outlet's amounts blank and adds the funds that are available for Ware House. Department totals can be partial. Check that all expected outlets have data before treating a total as complete.

Rounding can differ between backend values and Excel formulas at exact halfway amounts: Python round(2.5) gives 2, while Excel ROUND(2.5,0) gives 3. Floating-point precision can also affect a result.

What the Excel Formulas Do

06 / A practical reference for checking the calculation sheets

IDS Fund: follow one row

The first data row is row 5. C holds sales, D the category rate, E the category incentive, F the fund share and G the fund amount. Rates are stored as fractions: 30% is stored as 0.30.

E5 = IF(ISNUMBER(D5),ROUND(C5*D5,2),"Pending")
G5 = IF(AND(ISNUMBER(E5),ISNUMBER(F5)),
  ROUND(E5*F5,2),"Pending")

In ordinary language: calculate the amount only when the required rate or value is numeric. Otherwise, display Pending. The grand incentive and fund totals require every data row to contain a number.

IDS Fund Outlet Summary: add one outlet's rows

Column B adds the matching G cells from the IDS Fund sheet. For example:

='IDS Fund'!G5+'IDS Fund'!G6

This sheet starts its data on row 3. Adding a text Pending value directly can cause an Excel error, so check the source fund rows when an outlet total looks wrong.

Non-sales sheet: percentages first, amounts below

Rows 2-6 contain outlet fund links and allocation percentages. Row 7 contains the warehouse pool. Rows 11-15 calculate outlet department amounts; row 16 calculates Ware House. Central Service totals appear on row 18, with the HO subtotal first placed in G17.

C11 = IF(ISNUMBER(B2),ROUND(B2*C2,0),"Pending")
B7 = IF(COUNT(B2:B6)=5,SUM(B2:B6),"Pending")
D16 = IF(ISNUMBER(B7),ROUND(B7*D7,2),"Pending")

C is Accounts, D Ware House, E Customer Care, F Support and G HO. Other department calculations follow the same pattern as C11.

Open the workbook in Excel before relying on formulas
The exporter writes formulas but does not calculate them. Excel must recalculate the workbook. Formula-linked fund and non-sales sheets can update after edits; the basic and exact sales sheets remain the originally calculated values.

An upload with no recognized fund rows produces an unusual empty total range in the full workbook. Check this case rather than treating the output as a completed report.

A current full-workbook issue to check

HZT and GNG can receive the wrong linked fund
The fund summary lists all five outlets in the order ALM, ASH, GNG, HZT, VKN. The non-sales formula sheet labels them ALM, ASH, HZT, GNG, VKN but links by row number. With all five present, HZT and GNG therefore receive each other's fund. Missing outlets can shift other links as well.

The backend calculation matches funds by outlet name. The selected SALES / NON SALES / BOTH exports use those backend amounts. This guide identifies the full-workbook linking issue; no application code has been changed.

Why exports may differ

The full workbook's warehouse formula waits for five numeric outlet funds. The backend selected report uses funds that are available. Excel department totals may also omit Pending text and show partial totals. Rounding rules can produce further differences.

A simple final checklist

  1. Confirm that the file covers the period you intended.
  2. Match outlet sales to the original report.
  3. Check whether ACC COM explains a category-total difference.
  4. Confirm the profit, incentive and payout percentages.
  5. Check all expected outlet funds, especially HZT and GNG in the full workbook.
  6. Recalculate Excel and investigate Pending or error cells.
  7. Label the figures clearly: base incentive, exact sales payout or IDS Fund.

Technical reference: /api/incentive/export creates the full workbook. /api/incentive/selected-export creates sales, non-sales or both. /api/incentive/exact-export creates the sales report only.
